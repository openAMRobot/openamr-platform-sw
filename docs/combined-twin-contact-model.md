# Combined twin contact model — draft, 1 November 2026

The combined robot consumes this description package by reference. Contact surfaces must survive URDF-to-SDFormat conversion: they belong under `collision/surface`, and Bullet Featherstone reads its own friction fields. Fixed castor wheel bodies are preserved because gz-physics 7.6 assigns friction per body; lumping them into the chassis loses their low-friction contact approximation.

The ideal simulation uses isotropic tyre friction 1, chassis friction 0.2, frictionless fixed castor contacts, and zero rolling/spinning friction. These are explicit simulation estimates, not measured materials. The full robot also needs an explicitly configured ideal floor and a sufficiently small physics step in the consuming world's package. Bullet 3.24 combines spinning friction from both contact bodies; changing the robot alone is insufficient. The articulated model was checked at 1, 0.25 and 0.125 ms; the latter is the combined candidate. DART did not move the closing mimic grippers in the reproduced full model.

Castor housing boxes and tyre support spheres now consume the same CAD map as the visuals. The spheres use the CAD 17.5 mm radius and tyre centre; they approximate fixed, frictionless support rather than rolling/swivelling bearings. Existing joint frames and legacy inertials remain unchanged. Drive tyre width is 49.4 mm from the B2 STEP revolve profile. `tools/cad/export_component_geometry.py --check` verifies the generated reusable dimensions, including the button envelope consumed by the upperbody. No safety logic is represented by that button geometry.

Wheel state output is 50 Hz independently of the physics iteration rate, matching odometry's sampling frequency. The legacy RGB camera explicitly publishes CameraInfo to its already bridged `/camera/camera_info` topic. The Gemini RGB-D implementation from PR #53 remains intact. Near-field changes reuse the register-alignment subset of PR #103; no unrelated navigation or CI changes from that stack are replayed.

Validation on the draft source: 61 description checks, 15 driver/bringup introspection checks and 110 navigation-status tests passed. Reverting CAD contact geometry fails all six new dimension/support checks. Reverting the sensor wiring fails both new wiring checks. Contact conversion and near-field revert failures are retained in the integration evidence. These are not physical acceptance tests.

At the full-robot 0.125 ms checkpoint with corrected CAD contacts, measured forward/backward world displacement was +0.99910/-0.99877 m; odometry discrepancies were -0.086/-0.265 mm. The signed 90-degree turns were +88.29/-88.53 degrees. No matching 2.0 motion acceptance tolerance was found, so these are measurements, not formal acceptance. The final integration report must identify the exact consuming upperbody commit and rerun its checks.

Not verified: as-built wheel track, configured base mass/COM, real tyre/castor contact properties or load distribution, navigation/docking acceptance, physical/safety behavior. The 0.400 m track remains the approved placeholder; no Gate A measurement is substituted.
